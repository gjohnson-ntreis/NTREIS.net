Listing Entry & Update Timeline

Before72 hours to enter a new listing or required update.
Now3 business days, for new listings and any update: status, price, or terms.
Why it helpsMore breathing room. Weekends and holidays don't count, so a listing signed Friday afternoon isn't due Monday morning.

Non-Exclusive Listings

BeforeNon-exclusive listings were not accepted into the MLS.
NowAccepted for entry, as long as every other NTREIS requirement is met.
Why it helpsMore flexible listing options. Agents can get MLS exposure for sellers who want a non-exclusive arrangement.

Auction Listings

BeforeNo dedicated MLS provisions for auction properties.
NowA new Auction Listing field, required disclosures, and searchable/filterable entry.
Why it helpsBuyers' agents can find auction properties easily, and clear disclosures mean fewer surprises for everyone.

AI/Virtually Altered Images

BeforeNo dedicated MLS provision existed for altered images.
NowSpecific disclosure requirements for AI-generated or virtually altered images.
Why it helpsAgents can use virtual staging and photo enhancements with confidence, knowing exactly what's allowed.

Listing Do's & Don'ts

Public Remarks, Do

Enter helpful, factual detail: condition, updates, layout, HOA terms. Fields that aren't required are still worth filling in if they help a buyer evaluate the property. "Contact agent for more information" is fine on its own.

Public Remarks, Don't

No fair housing protected references, no direct contact information: names, phone numbers, emails, personal sites, URLs, or compensation/commission information.

Showings, Do

Reflect the seller's actual availability and access preferences, regardless of listing status. Update the moment it changes. Common notes include:

  • Future showing availability
  • Exterior/drive-by showings only
  • Showings subject to an accepted offer
  • Virtual showings only

Showings, Don't

Don't misrepresent showing availability in either direction, and don't leave outdated showing information in place after the seller's availability changes.

Key Facts

Questions you might have

Why is it called Data Quality instead of Compliance?

Compliance was always the enforcement side of a bigger goal: accurate, complete, and timely MLS data. Data Quality is the actual point, so that's what we're calling it. Alongside the rename, updated rules take effect November 1, and the full Rules Rewrite follows in January 2027. The name and the rules are both catching up to what the program has always been for.

When does NTREIS begin administering data quality?

November 1, 2026, as a soft launch focused on education.

What happens in January?

We'll publish the full NTREIS Rules Rewrite as a preview. The intent is the same, but the rules are consolidated so they're easier to find and understand. Webinars in January will walk you through them before they take effect February 1, 2027.

Is there a grace period?

Yes. From November 1, 2026, through January 31, 2027, NTREIS is focused on education, not fines, for everyday misses like a late entry or an outdated status. Full enforcement, including fines for non-egregious violations, begins February 1, 2027.

What doesn't get a grace period?

Egregious violations, like password sharing or using language prohibited under the settlement, are enforced immediately beginning November 1, 2026, with no correction window.

Can I still go to my association for listing corrections?

Yes, for everyday fixes like status, price, remarks, and other listing details. NTREIS can help with those too. For rules and policy questions, listing deletions, fraud reports, listing history changes, and Days on Market or contractual date updates, go to NTREIS directly at dataquality@ntreis.net. Every change needs a written request with supporting documentation.

Will brokers know if an agent gets a data quality notice?

Yes. Citation and correction notices go to the agent and to the broker or designated office manager together.

Can a citation be appealed?

Yes. You have 14 calendar days from the citation to file an appeal. Appeals are reviewed against a defined set of criteria: misapplication of a rule, a procedural error, or other extenuating circumstances.

Why is NTREIS handling this instead of my association?

Until now, each of the 13 member associations administered data quality independently, which led to inconsistent enforcement. Starting November 1, 2026, NTREIS is the single resource for data accuracy, potential violations, and data licensing across every market.
